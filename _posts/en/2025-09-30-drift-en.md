## The starting point

While watching the movie FOR THE FIRST TIME, two questions came to mind:

* *"Could I drift with my little VW Gol?"*
* *"Did the number of car rollovers go up after the movie came out?"*

We'll set the first question aside (for the sake of my little Gol), but the second one is a perfect starting point for a hypothesis test.

![Alt Text](https://media2.giphy.com/media/v1.Y2lkPTc5MGI3NjExODYyNXVlOTVqYmZiYjBndDVwcHIzeWx1ZWpuOXJzMzA2Y2s2dnN3NCZlcD12MV9pbnRlcm5hbF9naWZfYnlfaWQmY3Q9Zw/S6yhb6s2dKlfZMwop6/giphy.gif)

It all starts with a question. In our case:
**"Did the average number of rollovers increase after the movie's release?"**

---

## Defining the hypotheses

With the question defined, we create our hypotheses. I like to start with the **Alternative Hypothesis (Hₐ)** and then move to the **Null Hypothesis (H₀)**, so let's follow that order here. Hₐ is basically our suspicion, the thing we want to investigate.

* **Alternative Hypothesis (Hₐ):** The average number of rollovers increased after the movie's release.

H₀ is always the antagonist of our Hₐ, meaning we write it as the opposite of the alternative (we can also think of it as our "business as usual" :D)

* **Null Hypothesis (H₀):** The average number of rollovers stayed the same or decreased after the movie's release.

![Alt Text](https://media.giphy.com/media/v1.Y2lkPWVjZjA1ZTQ3Zm93bnlkcTFlM2YyeGY0bDk2bnNqc3h1azg0eHBoNmFhNmVxd2UzZyZlcD12MV9naWZzX3JlbGF0ZWQmY3Q9Zw/TFcwaBxd3lCQE/giphy.gif)

## Alpha (α) and p-value

From here on, our hypothesis test depends on two crucial values: **alpha (α)** and **the p-value**.

* **Alpha (α):** our threshold, the bar we measure against. It's a value set before the test (usually 0.05, or 5%).

  * It represents the **significance level**, that is, the maximum probability of making a **Type I Error** (rejecting H₀ by mistake, a "false positive").
  * Example: setting α = 0.05 means accepting a 5% chance of making that error.

* **p-value:** it's calculated from our data and works as a **surprise index**. It ranges from 0 to 1 and tells us how likely it would be to get these results if **H₀** were true.

  * **Low p-value (e.g., 0.01):** a very surprising result! Maybe our suspicion (Hₐ) is right.
  * **High p-value (e.g., 0.70):** nothing surprising; it could happen by chance, even if nothing had changed.

![Alt Text](https://media.giphy.com/media/v1.Y2lkPWVjZjA1ZTQ3bWZsZmw3aWFiMXd4ODd5aGZpaWR1ZnF5dm10cmh6c2pkdzJiZXJxcyZlcD12MV9naWZzX3NlYXJjaCZjdD1n/rrDuikSJh7Xi0/giphy.gif)

## Making the decision

The conclusion comes from comparing the p-value with α:

* If **p-value ≤ α**: we reject **H₀**.
  There is statistical evidence to support our suspicion.
  → In our example, we'd say yes, the movie's release seems to be related to the increase in rollover accidents.

* If **p-value > α**: we fail to reject **H₀**.
  We didn't find enough evidence to claim that the movie caused an increase.
  → We conclude there was no statistically significant change.

![Alt Text](https://media.giphy.com/media/v1.Y2lkPWVjZjA1ZTQ3bmU3OHZqNWdua3lyNmk0Zm92cDF5eHdqbm1ucHh6NDFlaWFtOWUybSZlcD12MV9naWZzX3JlbGF0ZWQmY3Q9Zw/8QC4kjqN9qDEk/giphy.gif)

## Testing it in practice with Python

If you wanted to run this analysis in **Python**, you'd probably use the **scipy** library. To compare the accidents "before" and "after", the ideal test would be the **Independent Samples T-Test**:

```python
from scipy import stats

# Assuming 'before' and 'after' are lists with the number of accidents
stats.ttest_ind(after, before, alternative='greater')
```

The `alternative='greater'` parameter specifies that we're running a **one-tailed test**, exactly like our original question!
